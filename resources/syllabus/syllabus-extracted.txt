PART 1
How to Use This Guide
What this document is, and the one distinction that matters.
What this is: a complete, organised checklist of the entire General Awareness (GA) syllabus for SSC CGL. The objective is that every
topic has a place — so you can systematically mark what you have learned, practised and revised, and never leave a gap by accident. 
Important distinction: SSC does not publish a chapter-by-chapter GA syllabus. Its official syllabus broadly specifies History, Culture,
Geography, Economic Scene, General Policy, Scientific Research, Current Events and everyday scientific awareness. The breakdown in
this guide expands those areas into a practical preparation syllabus based on what actually appears in the exam.

How to read each subject section: each subject is broken into sub-topics, and each sub-topic into micro-topics (the specific facts,
names, dates and concepts you must be able to recall). Tick each line as you complete it — that is your mastery checklist.

PART 2
Study Overview — 6 Months Syllabus + 2 Months Revision
& Mocks
A planning overview (not a rigid day-by-day plan) for the 8-month cycle.
THE OVERALL ARC
Months 1–6 — Syllabus completion: systematically cover all 8 subjects so that every topic on the checklist has been studied at
least once.
Months 7–8 — Revision + mock exams: no new topics; consolidate recall, build speed, and simulate the real exam through full-
length mock tests.
Rule of thumb: completion should feel ~80% done by the end of Month 6 — the last 20% of depth comes from revision, mocks and
mistake-analysis.
Monthly Rhythm — The Overview
Phase Months Focus
Foundation Month 1–2 Polity + History first (highest static-content weight); build the current-affairs daily
habit from day one.
Core build-up Month 3–4 Geography, Economics, General Science; current affairs now become second
nature.
Finishing & consolidation Month 5–6 Static GK & Culture, Environment & Ecology; complete remaining gaps; first round
of topic-wise mocks.
Revision + mocks Month 7–8 Full revision cycles, previous-year papers, full-length mock tests, speed +
accuracy drills, last-mile current affairs.
Weekly Rhythm — The Overview
Day type What the week looks like
6 study days 2–3 subjects per day in rotation; each session = new topic + immediate self-quiz. Rotate so every
subject is touched at least twice a week.
1 mock / test day One sectional or full mock test, followed by mandatory mistake analysis (note every wrong answer
in a mistakes register).
Daily non-negotiables 15–30 min current affairs (18-month lookback rule), 10 min static revision (one-liner recall), and one
previous-year question set.
Why this shape works: SSC GA rewards breadth of recall more than depth. A rotation that touches every subject weekly prevents
the "forgot everything after 2 weeks" problem, and the mock-day discipline builds exam temperament well before the real exam.

PART 3
Best Practices for Mastering General Awareness
T echniques that separate a clearing score from a mastery-level score.
1. The 80/20 Rule — Prioritise High-Yield Subjects
Rough exam weightage for GA in SSC CGL Tier-I (approximate): Polity and Constitution ~8–10 questions · Indian Economy &
Banking ~8–10 · History ~6–8 · Geography ~6–8 · General Science ~5–7 · Static GK & Culture ~6–8 · Environment ~3–5 · Current Affairs
~8–10.
Action: give Polity, Economy, History, Geography and Current Affairs the lion's share of your time; treat Environment and miscellaneous
static GK as quick-win topics. 
2. Learn — Recall — Verify (The Active Loop)
Learn a topic from a reliable source (one standard book + current notes).
Close the book and recall it in one line each — dates, names, facts.
Verify against the book and mark only what you got wrong.
Re-recall the wrong items 24 hours later, then 3 days later, then a week later.
Recall is the skill the exam actually tests — passive reading feels productive but does not build recall.
3. The One-Liner Notes System
Every fact you study should be reducible to one line (e.g., "Chilika Lake — largest brackish-water lagoon — Odisha — Ramsar site").
Maintain one subject-wise notebook/register; a topic is "done" only when it fits on a page you can revise in 10 minutes.
Prefer tables and comparisons for facts with many entries (dams, awards, capitals, revolutions).
4. Current Affairs — The 18-Month Rule
SSC asks current affairs roughly from the last 18 months before the exam (with emphasis on the last 10–12).
Daily: read/listen to a current-affairs digest; write one line per news item.
Monthly: compile and revise the month's lines; solve previous-year CA questions for the same period.
Track: government schemes (launch year, ministry, objective, beneficiaries, updates), appointments, awards, summits, and India's ranks/
indices.
5. Previous-Year Questions Are the Syllabus Map
Solve 5–8 years of CGL GA papers first — they tell you exactly which micro-topics repeat.
Mark every PYQ in this guide's checklist; the starred topics are your highest-return investments.
Never "skip" a topic that has appeared even once in the last decade.
6. Mock Tests — The Exam Simulator
From Month 5: one full mock test weekly (strictly timed, 60 minutes for Tier-I GA+other sections).
From Month 7: two to three full mocks weekly; sectional tests for GA every 3–4 days.
Mistakes register: every wrong answer → what was the correct fact → where it sits in this syllabus → re-learn within 24 hours.
Speed discipline: GA should take ~12–15 minutes in Tier-I; practise rounding, elimination and marking-and-returning tactics.
7. Revision Cadence (Spaced Repetition)
The 3-2-1 rule: revise every topic 3 times — after 24 hours, after 3 days, after a week. A topic revised three times within the cycle is
far more likely to stay in memory than one studied deeply once. 
8. Exam-Day Cleverness
Eliminate first: SSC GA loves "none of these" and close-option traps; narrow to two choices before guessing.
Attempt in two passes: easy recall questions first; mark and return to ambiguous ones.

CLOSING NOTE
Your Path to GST Inspector
Final reminders for the 8-month cycle.
1. Breadth first, depth second. SSC GA rewards how much you can recall, not how deep you know one topic. Finish the entire
checklist before going deep on any single subject. 
2. Consistency beats intensity. 2 hours daily + 30 minutes of current affairs beats a 10-hour weekend binge. The 3-2-1 revision rule
keeps everything in memory. 
3. Mocks are not optional. From Month 5, the mistakes register is your most valuable book. Every wrong answer is a fact you will
never forget again. 
4. Target: mastery, not minimum. A GST Inspector post demands a strong Tier-I score and solid Tier-II performance. Aim to answer GA
in under 15 minutes with 85%+ accuracy. 
Remember: SSC does not publish a chapter-by-chapter GA syllabus. This guide expands the official broad categories into a practical,
complete preparation map. Treat it as your master checklist — every topic has a place, and every place can be ticked.
