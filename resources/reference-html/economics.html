⚖️ Demand Elasticity & Anomalous Material Goods

📉 Fundamental Law of Demand

Price Acceleration ($P \uparrow$) leads to Quantity Contraction ($Q \downarrow$). Curve maintains a negative downward slope. Supply Corollary: Price Acceleration ($P \uparrow$) prompts Output Expansion ($S \uparrow$).

🎩 Structural Violations of Demand Law

  • Giffen Commodities: Non-luxury inferior base goods — Price acceleration triggers quantity consumption increases (Observed by Sir Robert Giffen).
  • Veblen Commodities: Positional/Snob status markers (e.g., Rare cut diamonds 💎).

📐 Elasticity Coefficients

  • Inelastic Absolute Necessities (e.g., Edible Halite / Salt) $\rightarrow \epsilon < 1$.

👥 Socio-Economic Indicators, Poverty & Human Development

📉 Structural Typologies of Unemployment

  • Disguised Unemployment: Labor deployment exceeding real technical requirements (Highly prevalent in the agrarian primary sector 🌾). Marginal Productivity ($MP$) of surplus labor approaches zero ($MP_L \to 0$).
  • Structural: Long-run mismatch between labor force skills and evolving corporate demand profiles.
  • Frictional: Short-run transition periods during worker job changes.
  • Cyclical: Driven by macroeconomic contractions and recessions (Characteristic of capitalist economic engines).

    🌍 External Sector Dynamics & Global Financial Multilaterals

    Balance of Payments Accounts · Exchange Rate Paradigms · Bretton Woods Agreements · Trade Frameworks

    ⚖️ Balance of Payments (BoP) Accounting Matrix

    • Current Account Vector: Tracks visible merchandise trade (Balance of Trade), invisible services, and unilateral transfer streams.
    • Capital Account Vector: Captures Foreign Direct Investment (FDI), Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI), and external sovereign borrowing.
    • Balance of Trade (BoT) isolates visible merchandise transactions only ($$\text{Exports} - \text{Imports}$$).
    • India's largest single net export earner is the software services sector.

    💱 Exchange Rate Mechanics

    • Fixed Pegged Regime vs Market-Driven Floating Regime (Adopted post-1991 market transition).
    • Managed Float System: Active central bank intervention to curb extreme currency volatility.
    • Sovereign currency depreciation makes domestic exports more competitive while increasing import costs.
    • India maintains full convertibility on the Current Account, but partial convertibility on the Capital Account.
