📌 Types of Motion

Linear / Rectilinear

  • Motion in a straight line
  • Ex: Car on highway, falling stone

Circular Motion

  • Motion in a circular path
  • Ex: Fan blades, Earth revolving Sun

Oscillatory / Periodic

  • Back and forth motion
  • Ex: Pendulum, guitar string

Projectile Motion

  • Combination of horizontal + vertical
  • Ex: Cricket ball, cannon ball

🌍 Gravity & Free Fall

Key Values

  • g = 9.8 m/s² (gravitational acceleration on Earth)
  • g is maximum at poles, minimum at equator
  • g decreases with altitude & depth
  • g on Moon = 1/6th of Earth

Momentum

  • p = mv (mass × velocity)
  • SI unit: kg·m/s
  • Conservation of momentum: Total momentum before = after (no external force)
  • Example: Gun recoil

📏 Key Definitions & SI Units
Electric Current (I)Rate of flow of charge. Unit: Ampere (A)
Voltage / EMF (V)Potential difference. Unit: Volt (V)
Resistance (R)Opposition to current. Unit: Ohm (Ω)
Power (P)Rate of doing work. Unit: Watt (W)
EnergyP × time. Unit: Joule (J) or kWh

Conductance1/R. Unit: Siemens (S) or Mho

📐 Ohm's Law
OHM'S LAW (V = IR)
V = I × R   |   I = V/R   |   R = V/I
P = VI = I²R = V²/R   |   E = P × t (Energy = Power × Time)

"VIR Triangle" — Cover what you need:
Cover V → I × R  |  Cover I → V/R  |  Cover R → V/I
Remember: "Very Important Relationship" → V = IR

🔗 Series vs Parallel Circuits
📏 Series Circuit
  • Same current through all components
  • Voltage divides: V = V₁ + V₂ + V₃
  • Total Resistance: R = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ (increases)
  • If one bulb fails → ALL go off
  • Example: Old Christmas lights
🔀 Parallel Circuit
  • Same voltage across all components
  • Current divides: I = I₁ + I₂ + I₃
  • Total Resistance: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ (decreases)
  • If one bulb fails → others stay on
  • Example: Home wiring (preferred!)
🔌 AC vs DC
🔵 AC (Alternating Current)
  • Direction changes periodically
  • Frequency: 50 Hz in India
  • Generated by alternators
  • Used in homes & industries
  • Easier to transmit over long distances
🔴 DC (Direct Current)
  • Flows in one direction only
  • Generated by batteries & cells
  • Used in electronics, mobiles
  • Harder to transmit long distances
  • Thomas Edison championed DC
🧲 Magnetic Effects of Current

Electromagnet

  • Created when current flows through a coil
  • Strength increases with more turns & current
  • Used in: electric bells, cranes, MRI machines

Key Scientists

  • Oersted — Current produces magnetic field
  • Faraday — Electromagnetic Induction
  • Fleming's Rule — Left hand: motor; Right hand: generator

Electric Devices

  • Motor: converts electrical → mechanical energy
  • Generator/Dynamo: mechanical → electrical
  • Transformer: changes AC voltage levels
  • Fuse: safety device (thin wire melts at excess current)

🔵 Conduction
  • Transfer through direct contact
  • Occurs mainly in solids
  • Best conductor: Silver, Copper
  • Worst conductors: Wood, Air, Wool
  • Example: Spoon in hot tea gets warm
🟠 Convection
  • Transfer through fluid movement
  • Occurs in liquids & gases
  • Hot air rises, cool air sinks
  • Example: Sea breeze, boiling water
  • Used in: Radiators, AC systems

☀️ Radiation

  • Transfer without any medium (vacuum possible)
  • Travels at speed of light (3 × 10⁸ m/s)
  • Example: Heat from Sun reaching Earth
  • Black bodies absorb and emit most radiation
  • Thermos flask uses vacuum to prevent radiation

Remember modes of heat transfer:
"ConCrete Roads" → Conduction (Contact) · Convection (Currents) · Radiation
CCR — Conduction, Convection, Radiation

💧 Specific Heat & Latent Heat

Specific Heat Capacity

  • Heat needed to raise 1 kg by 1°C
  • Water has highest specific heat = 4200 J/kg·K
  • This is why coastal areas have moderate climate
  • Formula: Q = mcΔT

Latent Heat

  • Heat absorbed/released during change of state without change in temperature
  • Latent heat of fusion (ice to water): 336 kJ/kg
  • Latent heat of vaporisation (water to steam): 2260 kJ/kg
  • Why burns from steam are worse than boiling water!

⚖️ Metals vs Non-Metals: Key Comparison
🔩 Metals
  • Lustrous (shiny) appearance
  • Good conductors of heat & electricity
  • Malleable (can be beaten into sheets)
  • Ductile (can be drawn into wires)
  • High melting/boiling point (except Mercury, Gallium)
  • Solid at room temp (except Mercury — liquid)
  • Form basic oxides (react with acid)
  • Produce metallic sound (sonorous)
🌿 Non-Metals
  • Non-lustrous (dull) — except Iodine, Diamond
  • Poor conductors (except Graphite)
  • Brittle — cannot be beaten into sheets
  • Not ductile
  • Low melting/boiling point (generally)
  • Exist as solids, liquids & gases at room temp
  • Form acidic oxides
  • Not sonorous

🦠 Corrosion & Rusting

🟤 Rusting of Iron

  • Rusting = Iron + Oxygen + Water → Iron Oxide (Fe₂O₃·xH₂O)
  • Conditions required: Moisture (water) + Oxygen
  • Salt water accelerates rusting (electrolyte)
  • Prevention: Painting, Galvanizing, Oiling, Alloying

🔵 Galvanization

  • Coating iron with Zinc (Zn)
  • Zinc sacrificially corrodes to protect iron
  • Used in: buckets, pipes, roofing sheets

⏱️ History of Periodic Table
1817 — Döbereiner's Triads
Groups of 3 elements with middle element having avg. atomic mass of other two
1866 — Newlands' Octaves
Every 8th element has similar properties to first (failed for heavier elements)
1869 — Mendeleev's Table
Arranged elements by atomic mass; predicted undiscovered elements (Eka-aluminium = Gallium)
1913 — Modern Periodic Table (Moseley)
Arranged by atomic number (not mass). Modern Periodic Law: Properties are periodic function of atomic number
📐 Structure of Modern Periodic Table

Periods (Rows)

  • Total: 7 Periods
  • Period number = Number of electron shells
  • Period 1: 2 elements (H, He)
  • Period 2 & 3: 8 elements each
  • Period 4 & 5: 18 elements each
  • Period 6: 32 elements

Groups (Columns)

  • Total: 18 Groups
  • Group number = Valence electrons (for main groups)
  • Group 1: Alkali Metals (Li, Na, K...)
  • Group 17: Halogens (F, Cl, Br...)
  • Group 18: Noble Gases (He, Ne, Ar...)
  • Groups 3–12: Transition Metals

❤️ Circulatory System (Most Tested!)

Heart

  • 4 chambers: 2 Atria + 2 Ventricles
  • Pumps ~5 litres of blood per minute
  • Normal heart rate: 60–100 beats/min
  • Pacemaker = SA Node (Sinoatrial node)
  • Largest artery: Aorta | Smallest: Capillaries

Blood

  • Volume: ~5–6 litres in adult human
  • RBC (Red Blood Cells) — carry O₂ via Haemoglobin
  • WBC (White Blood Cells) — fight infection (immunity)
  • Platelets — clotting of blood
  • Plasma — liquid part (~55% of blood)

Blood Groups (ABO System)

  • Blood groups: A, B, AB, O
  • O = Universal Donor
  • AB = Universal Recipient
  • Rh factor: Rh+ (positive) or Rh− (negative)
  • Discovered by: Karl Landsteiner

🍎 Macronutrients Overview

🌾 Carbohydrates

  • Primary energy source (4 kcal/g)
  • Glucose, Fructose, Sucrose, Starch
  • Stored as Glycogen in liver & muscles
  • Sources: Rice, wheat, bread, sugar

🥩 Proteins

  • Body building nutrients (4 kcal/g)
  • Made of Amino Acids (20 types)
  • Essential amino acids (must come from food): 9
  • Sources: Eggs, meat, pulses, soya
  • Deficiency → Kwashiorkor, Marasmus
