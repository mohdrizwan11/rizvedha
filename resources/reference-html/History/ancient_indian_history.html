Prehistoric India
500000 BCE – 2500 BCE · Stone Age to Chalcolithic

🏔️ Paleolithic Age (Old Stone Age) — 500000–10000 BCE

  • Also called: Quartzite Age (tools made of quartzite)
  • Three phases: Lower, Middle, Upper Paleolithic
  • People were hunter-gatherers, no agriculture
  • Tools: hand axes, cleavers, choppers
  • Important sites: Soan/Sohan Valley (Punjab/Pakistan), Belan Valley (Mirzapur, UP), Narmada Valley, Tungabhadra
  • Narmada Man — earliest human fossil found in India
  • Ice Age / Pleistocene period

🌿 Mesolithic Age (Middle Stone Age) — 10000–6000 BCE

  • Transitional phase — warmer climate after Ice Age
  • Tools: Microliths (tiny blades/tools)
  • Earliest evidence of domestication of animals
  • Rock paintings: Bhimbetka (MP) — UNESCO World Heritage Site
  • Sites: Langhnaj (Gujarat), Bagor (Rajasthan), Adamgarh (MP)
  • Semi-nomadic lifestyle, fishing & hunting

🌾 Neolithic Age (New Stone Age) — 6000–2500 BCE

  • Beginning of agriculture & settled life
  • Tools: polished stone tools
  • Pottery making begins
  • First evidence of wheel
  • Key sites: Mehrgarh (Baluchistan — earliest Neolithic, 7000 BCE), Burzahom (Kashmir), Chirand (Bihar), Piklihal (Karnataka)
  • Mehrgarh — earliest evidence of farming in South Asia
  • Cattle & sheep domestication

🥉 Chalcolithic Age (Copper-Stone Age) — 3000–1200 BCE

  • Use of both copper and stone
  • First use of metal in India
  • Sites: Ahar (Rajasthan), Navdatoli (MP), Inamgaon (Maharashtra), Jorwe culture
  • Kayatha culture (MP) and Malwa culture
  • Overlap with IVC period
  • Burial practices begin (dead buried under floors)

Indus Valley Civilization (IVC)
2600–1900 BCE · Bronze Age Urban Civilization

⭐ Key Facts at a Glance

  • Also called Harappan Civilization (first site discovered = Harappa)
  • Discovered in 1921 by Dayaram Sahni (Harappa) & 1922 by RD Banerjee (Mohenjo-daro)
  • Largest Bronze Age civilization in the world
  • Period: 2600–1900 BCE (Mature phase)
  • Script: undeciphered boustrophedon (written right to left, then left to right)
  • Covered: Punjab, Sindh, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Haryana, UP, Maharashtra

🏗️ Town Planning — Most Asked!

  • Grid pattern streets — East-West & North-South
  • Two-storey burnt brick houses
  • Great Bath — Mohenjo-daro (world's first public bath)
  • Elaborate underground drainage system
  • Granaries (largest: Mohenjo-daro)
  • Separate Citadel (upper town) & lower town
  • No stone buildings (unlike Egypt/Mesopotamia)

🏺 Economy & Trade

  • Trade with Mesopotamia (Sumer) — called Meluhha
  • Weights & measures: binary system (1, 2, 4, 8...)
  • Standard cuboid weights made of chert
  • Cotton cultivation — first in the world
  • No temples found (unique!)
  • Merchants class dominated

📍 Major Sites — Must Remember!

  • Harappa — Punjab, Pakistan; first discovered; R-37 cemetery
  • Mohenjo-daro — Sindh, Pakistan; "Mound of the Dead"; Great Bath
  • Dholavira — Gujarat, India; unique 3-part city; UNESCO (2021)
  • Kalibangan — Rajasthan; earliest plowed field & fire altars
  • Lothal — Gujarat; first dock/port in world; rice cultivation
  • Rakhigarhi — Haryana; largest IVC site in India
  • Surkotada — Gujarat; horse bones found
  • Banawali — Haryana; toy plough found
  • Chanhudaro — Sindh; inkpot & cartwright found

🎨 Art & Artefacts

  • Dancing Girl — bronze statue, Mohenjo-daro
  • Priest-King — steatite sculpture, Mohenjo-daro
  • Pashupati seal — proto-Shiva seated in yogic posture
  • Unicorn seal — most common animal seal
  • Terracotta figurines (Mother Goddess)
  • Beads of carnelian, gold, lapis lazuli

⚠️ Decline of IVC — Theories

  • Aryan invasion theory (outdated)
  • Climate change & drought
  • Drying up of Ghaggar-Hakra (Saraswati) river
  • Floods (Mohenjo-daro)
  • Environmental degradation
  • Most accepted: Multiple factors

🎯 SSC CGL Top PYQs — IVC

  • Who discovered Harappa? → Dayaram Sahni (1921)
  • Great Bath is located at → Mohenjo-daro
  • First dock in world → Lothal
  • Earliest plowed field → Kalibangan
  • IVC script direction → Right to left (boustrophedon)
  • IVC trade with → Mesopotamia (Sumer)
  • Dancing Girl found at → Mohenjo-daro
  • Largest IVC site in India → Rakhigarhi
  • Cotton cultivation started first → IVC (India)

Vedic Age
1500–600 BCE · Early Vedic (1500–1000 BCE) & Later Vedic (1000–600 BCE)

📚 The Four Vedas — Core!

  • Rigveda — Oldest Veda; hymns to gods; 1028 suktas (hymns); Gayatri Mantra
  • Samaveda — Songs/melodies; basis of Indian classical music
  • Yajurveda — Rituals & sacrificial formulas
  • Atharvaveda — Magic spells, charms, medicine; Brahma priests

📖 Vedic Literature Structure

  • Vedas (Shruti) — Samhitas (hymns), Brahmanas, Aranyakas, Upanishads
  • Upanishads (108) — philosophical texts; "end of Vedas" = Vedanta
  • Puranas (18) — oldest: Matsya Purana
  • Epics — Ramayana (Valmiki), Mahabharata (Vyasa; largest epic in world)
  • Vedangas (6) — Shiksha, Kalpa, Vyakarana, Nirukta, Chhanda, Jyotish
  • Dharmashastras — Manusmriti (laws of Manu)

🌅 Early Vedic Society (1500–1000 BCE)

  • Aryans settled in Saptasindhu (land of 7 rivers)
  • Economy: pastoral (cattle = wealth)
  • Tribal society; chief = Rajan
  • Sabha & Samiti — early democratic assemblies
  • Varna based on occupation (not birth)
  • Indra most important god (rain/war)
  • River Saraswati most sacred
  • Women had high status (Gargi, Lopamudra, Maitreyi)

🌆 Later Vedic Society (1000–600 BCE)

  • Eastward expansion to Ganga plains
  • Agriculture became primary occupation
  • Varna became birth-based (rigid caste)
  • Prajapati/Vishnu/Shiva rose in importance
  • 16 Mahajanapadasbegin to emerge
  • Iron tools used for agriculture
  • Women's status declined
  • Capital: Hastinapur (Kuru), Kaushambi (Vatsa)

🏛️ Vedic Polity — Key Terms

Rashtra = State Rajan = Chief/King Sabha = Upper assembly Samiti = Lower assembly Vidatha = Oldest assembly Gramani = Village head Purohita = Royal priest Senani = Army chief

🎯 SSC Hot Questions — Vedic Age

  • Oldest Veda → Rigveda
  • Gayatri Mantra is in → Rigveda
  • Veda of music → Samaveda
  • Mahabharata author → Vyasa
  • Ramayana author → Valmiki
  • Most important god in Early Vedic = Indra
  • Upanishads deal with → Philosophy
  • Sabha & Samiti mentioned in → Atharvaveda

🏰 Rise of Magadha — Dynasties

  • Haryanka dynasty (544–413 BCE) — Bimbisara (founder; first to have standing army), Ajatashatru
  • Shishunaga dynasty (413–345 BCE) — destroyed Avanti
  • Nanda dynasty (345–321 BCE) — Mahapadma Nanda (first Shudra king); vast empire; Dhana Nanda (last)
  • Advantages: iron resources, fertile land, river routes, strategic location

🌍 Foreign Invasions (6th–4th c BCE)

  • Darius I — annexed NW India; inscriptions at Persepolis
  • Alexander the Great (326 BCE) — Battle of Hydaspes (Jhelum); defeated Porus; turned back at Beas (Hyphasis) mutiny
  • Impact: new trade routes, Hellenistic art (Gandhara school)

Buddhism & Jainism
6th Century BCE — Most asked in SSC!

☸️ Buddhism — Complete Notes

  • Founder: Siddhartha Gautama (563–483 BCE)
  • Born: Lumbini (Nepal) — Kshatriya clan of Shakyas
  • Enlightenment: Bodh Gaya (under Peepal/Bodhi tree)
  • First sermon: Sarnath (Deer Park) = Dhammachakkapavattana
  • Death (Mahaparinirvana): Kushinagar
  • Four Noble Truths (Arya Satya): Life is suffering → Cause is desire → Cessation → Eightfold Path
  • Eightfold Path (Ashtangika Marga): Right View, Thought, Speech, Action, Livelihood, Effort, Mindfulness, Concentration
  • Tripitaka: Vinaya, Sutta, Abhidhamma Pitaka
  • Two sects: Hinayana (strict) & Mahayana (liberal)
  • Buddhist councils: 1st Rajgriha (483 BCE), 2nd Vaishali (383 BCE), 3rd Pataliputra (250 BCE) — Ashoka, 4th Kashmir (1st c CE) — Kanishka

🔱 Jainism — Complete Notes

  • 24 Tirthankaras (teachers) — Rishabhadeva (first), Mahavira (last/24th)
  • Mahavira (599–527 BCE) — also called Vardhamana
  • Born: Vaishali (Bihar); Kshatriya (Lichhavi)
  • Enlightenment: Jrimbhikagrama (near Rajgir)
  • Death: Pavapuri (Bihar)
  • Five Vows (Pancha Mahavrata): Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya, Brahmacharya, Aparigraha
  • Three Jewels (Triratna): Right Faith, Right Knowledge, Right Conduct
  • Two sects: Digambara (sky-clad) & Shvetambara (white-clad)
  • Council: 1st Pataliputra (3rd BCE), 2nd Valabhi (5th CE)
  • Jain texts: Agamas

🔍 Buddhism vs Jainism — Key Differences

  • Ahimsa: Both follow, but Jainism is extreme
  • God: Buddhism — no personal God; Jainism — no creator God
  • Soul: Buddhism — no soul (Anatman); Jainism — soul exists (Jiva)
  • Texts: Buddhism = Pali; Jainism = Prakrit/Ardhamagadhi
  • Women: Buddhism accepted in Sangha; Jainism — Digambara no, Shvetambara yes

🏛️ Buddhist Architecture

  • Stupa — relic mound; Sanchi (Ashoka), Bharhut, Amaravati
  • Vihara — monastery (Nalanda)
  • Chaitya — prayer hall (Karla caves)
  • Ajanta — cave paintings (1st c BCE – 6th CE)
  • Ellora — Buddhist, Hindu, Jain caves
  • Gandhara art — Greek influence (Buddha shown in human form)
  • Mathura art — indigenous (earthy; partially clothed)
  • Amaravati art — finest sculptures

🎯 SSC Hot Questions — Buddhism & Jainism

  • Birthplace of Buddha → Lumbini | Mahavira → Vaishali
  • Buddha's first sermon at → Sarnath ("Deer Park")
  • Language of Buddhist texts → Pali
  • 3rd Buddhist Council patron → Ashoka
  • 4th Buddhist Council patron → Kanishka
  • Founder of Jainism → Rishabhadeva | Reformer → Mahavira
  • Two Jain sects → Digambara & Shvetambara
  • Pali tripitaka has → 3 pitakas
  • Bodhi tree species → Ficus religiosa (Peepal)

Mauryan Empire
322–185 BCE · India's First Great Empire
Chandragupta Maurya
322–298 BCE
Founded empire; defeated Nanda dynasty with help of Chanakya/Kautilya; defeated Seleucus Nicator (305 BCE); adopted Jainism; died at Shravanabelagola (fasting)
Bindusara
298–268 BCE
"Amitraghata" (slayer of enemies); expanded to Deccan; maintained Greek diplomatic relations (Megasthenes' successor Deimachus); son = Ashoka
Ashoka the Great
268–232 BCE
Greatest Mauryan; Kalinga War (261 BCE) → converted to Buddhism; Dhamma policy; built stupas; sent missionaries (son Mahendra to Sri Lanka); pillar edicts

📋 Mauryan Administration — Key Points

  • Capital: Pataliputra (Patna)
  • King aided by Mantri Parishad (council of ministers)
  • Provinces: 4 major provinces with royal princes as governors
  • Districts (Ahara/Vishaya) → villages
  • Source: Arthashastra (Kautilya) & Indica (Megasthenes)
  • Megasthenes: Greek ambassador; described 7 castes in India
  • Spy system: Gudhapurusha
  • Currency: Pana (silver) & Mashaka

🗿 Ashoka's Edicts — Must Know!

  • 14 Major Rock Edicts — Dhamma principles
  • 7 Pillar Edicts — administration & policy
  • Minor rock edicts + cave inscriptions
  • Script: Brahmi (most) & Kharosthi (NW India)
  • Language: Prakrit (mostly); Greek & Aramaic (NW)
  • Allahabad Pillar — Samudragupta's feats also inscribed
  • Rock Edict XIII — Kalinga war & repentance
  • Brahmi deciphered by James Prinsep (1837)

🏛️ Ashoka's Dhamma (Policy of Righteousness)

  • Not a religion but a code of conduct (ethical conduct)
  • Respect for all religions; tolerance; non-violence
  • Ban on animal sacrifice; welfare works (hospitals, roads, wells)
  • Sent Dharma Mahamatra as officers for Dhamma
  • Son Mahendra → Sri Lanka; daughter Sanghamitra → Sri Lanka with Bodhi tree sapling

🏛️ Mauryan Art & Architecture

  • Lion Capital (Sarnath) — National Emblem of India
  • Ashoka Chakra (24-spoked wheel) — on National Flag
  • Sanchi Stupa — built by Ashoka
  • Barabar caves — earliest rock-cut caves in India
  • Pillars: monolithic, polished sandstone
  • Pataliputra palace — described by Megasthenes as magnificent

📉 Decline of Mauryan Empire

  • Weak successors after Ashoka
  • Economic strain (large army & gifts)
  • Brahmin reaction to Ashoka's Buddhism
  • Last ruler: Brihadratha (killed by Pushyamitra Shunga, 185 BCE)
  • Provincial governors became independent

Post-Mauryan Period
185 BCE – 320 CE · Sungas, Kushans, Satavahanas & Sangam Age

🏰 Shunga Dynasty (185–73 BCE)

  • Founder: Pushyamitra Shunga — Brahmin general who killed Brihadratha
  • Revived Brahminism; performed Ashvamedha (horse sacrifice)
  • Patronized Sanchi stupa (gateway carvings)
  • Fought with Yavanas (Greeks) — Menander's invasion
  • Patanjali wrote Mahabhashya (grammar) under Shungas
  • Last ruler: Devabhuti — killed by Kanva minister Vasudeva

🌟 Kushana Dynasty (1st–3rd c CE)

  • Central Asian tribe; conquered NW India
  • Greatest king: Kanishka (78 CE — started Saka era?)
  • Capital: Purushapura (Peshawar)
  • Patron of Buddhism; 4th Buddhist Council
  • Court: Ashvaghosha (poet, Buddhacharita), Charaka (medicine), Nagarjuna (Buddhist philosopher)
  • Gandhara & Mathura school of art flourished

🌾 Satavahana Empire (1st c BCE–3rd c CE)

  • Deccan-based; longest ruling dynasty in ancient India
  • Greatest king: Gautamiputra Satkarni
  • Defeated Shakas (Western Kshatrapas)
  • Capital: Pratishthan/Paithan (Maharashtra)
  • Promoted Prakrit language (Gathasaptasati by Hala)
  • Issued coins with bilingual inscriptions

🎵 Sangam Age (3rd BCE–3rd CE)

  • Tamil kingdoms: Chera, Chola, Pandya
  • Three Sangams (literary academies) at Madurai
  • Sangam literature: Tolkappiyam (earliest Tamil grammar), Purananuru, Silappathikaram
  • Trade with Rome — "Pepper for Roman gold"
  • Ports: Muzuris (Muchiri), Puhar/Kaveripattinam
  • Silappadikaram & Manimekalai — twin epics

🌍 Indo-Greeks & Other Foreign Rulers

  • Indo-Greeks — first to issue gold coins in India; Menander (Milinda) — dialogue with Buddhist monk Nagasena in Milindapanha
  • Shakas (Scythians) — Rudradaman I (best Sanskrit inscription at Junagadh); patronized Sanskrit
  • Parthians — Gondophernes (during whose reign St. Thomas came to India)

🎯 SSC Hot Questions — Post-Mauryan

  • Shunga dynasty founder → Pushyamitra Shunga
  • Greatest Kushana king → Kanishka
  • 4th Buddhist Council organized by → Kanishka
  • Gandhara art shows → Greek influence on Buddha images
  • Satavahana greatest king → Gautamiputra Satkarni
  • Milindapanha is dialogue between → Menander & Nagasena
  • Oldest Tamil grammar → Tolkappiyam
  • Nagarjuna was → Buddhist philosopher (Madhyamika school)

Gupta Empire — Golden Age of India
320–550 CE · Greatest cultural flowering
Chandragupta I
320–335 CE
Founded Gupta Empire; started Gupta Era (319–320 CE); married Licchhavi princess Kumaradevi; title "Maharajadhiraja"
Samudragupta
335–380 CE
"Napoleon of India" (Vincent A. Smith); military genius; Allahabad Pillar Inscription (by Harishena); played veena; Ashvamedha yajna
Chandragupta II "Vikramaditya"
380–415 CE
Golden Age peak; defeated Shakas; Fa Hien visited (399–414 CE); Navratnas in court including Kalidasa; Iron Pillar at Delhi
Kumaragupta I
415–455 CE
Founded Nalanda University; maintained empire; faced Pushyamitra attacks
Skandagupta
455–467 CE
Repelled Huna invasions; last great Gupta king; after him, rapid decline

🔬 Science & Mathematics — Gupta

  • Aryabhatta — value of π (3.14); Earth rotates; zero concept; Aryabhatiya
  • Varahamihira — Brihat Samhita (astronomy, astrology)
  • Brahmagupta — rules for zero and negative numbers
  • Charaka — Charaka Samhita (medicine)
  • Sushruta — Sushruta Samhita (surgery); plastic surgery pioneer
  • Dhanvantari — court physician; Ayurveda

📚 Literature — Gupta

  • Kalidasa (greatest poet) — Shakuntala, Meghaduta, Kumarasambhava, Raghuvamsha, Malavikagnimitra, Vikramorvasiya
  • Vishakhadatta — Mudrarakshasa (play on Chandragupta Maurya)
  • Sudraka — Mrichchhakatika (Clay Cart)
  • Amarasimha — Amarakosha (Sanskrit dictionary)
  • Panchatantra (Vishnu Sharma) — fables
  • Fa Hien's account — Chinese pilgrim; traveled 399–414 CE

🎨 Art & Architecture — Gupta

  • Temples: Dashavatara Temple (Deogarh), Bhitargaon temple
  • Iron Pillar (Delhi) — no rust for 1600 years; Chandragupta II
  • Ajanta caves (paintings) — peak under Guptas
  • Mathura & Sarnath school — pure indigenous style
  • Nagara style temples (North India)
  • Gupta coins — excellent craftsmanship

🏛️ Gupta Administration

  • More decentralized than Mauryan
  • Feudal tendencies began
  • Revenue: 1/6th of produce
  • Land grants (Agrahara) to Brahmins
  • Chinese traveler Fa Hien praised law & order
  • No capital punishment for ordinary crimes
  • Nalanda University (Bihar) — world's first residential university

Kalidasa (poet) Varahamihira (astronomer) Dhanvantari (physician) Vetalbhatta (magician) Amarasimha (lexicographer) Vararuchi Ksapanaka Shanku Ghatakarpar

Post-Gupta Period
550–750 CE · Harsha, Chalukyas, Pallavas

👑 Harsha (606–647 CE) — Key Figure

  • King of Thanesar; united much of North India
  • Capital: Kanauj
  • Defeated by Pulakesi II (Chalukya) on Narmada
  • Chinese pilgrim Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang) visited his court
  • Harsha wrote: Nagananda, Ratnavali, Priyadarshika
  • Court poet Bana wrote Harshacharita (biography) & Kadambari
  • Patronized both Buddhism & Shaivism
  • Organized Kumbh Mela at Prayag (every 5 years)

🏰 Chalukyas of Badami (543–753 CE)

  • Founder: Pulakesi I (543 CE)
  • Greatest: Pulakesi II — defeated Harsha; contemporary of Hiuen Tsang
  • Capital: Vatapi/Badami (Karnataka)
  • Hiuen Tsang visited Chalukya court
  • Aihole inscription — by court poet Ravikirti
  • Rock-cut temples at Badami, Aihole, Pattadakal
  • Pattadakal — UNESCO World Heritage Site

🏛️ Pallava Dynasty (4th–9th c CE)

  • South Indian dynasty; capital Kanchipuram
  • Greatest king: Mahendravarman I and Narasimhavarman I
  • Shore Temple at Mamallapuram (Mahabalipuram)
  • Pancha Rathas (5 monolithic temples)
  • Dravidian temple architecture — pioneered
  • Hiuen Tsang visited Kanchipuram
  • Defeated Chalukyas multiple times

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • IVC = Harappan civilization (NOT Dravidian — not proven)
  • Mahavira ≠ founder of Jainism — he was 24th Tirthankara; Rishabhadeva was first
  • Ashoka Rock Edict XIII ≠ Allahabad Pillar — different inscriptions!
  • Gupta Era starts 319–320 CE, NOT with Samudragupta
  • "Napoleon of India" = Samudragupta NOT Chandragupta II
  • Saka Era (78 CE) ≠ Gupta Era (319 CE)
  • Bhimbetka = Mesolithic (NOT Paleolithic)
  • Lothal = first dock, NOT first IVC site discovered
