The Tripartite Struggle for Kannauj
8th–10th Century CE
🔥 HIGH YIELD

🗺 Why Kannauj? The Prize City

Kannauj (in modern Uttar Pradesh) was the most prestigious city in India — "the city of the Lord." Controlling Kannauj meant supremacy over North India. Three powerful empires fought for it:

🦅 Gurjara-Pratiharas
North India (Rajasthan + UP)
  • Founder: Nagabhata I
  • Greatest ruler: Mihira Bhoja (836–885)
  • Capital: Kannauj
  • Arab traveler Al-Masudi called them the richest kingdom
  • Strongest barrier against Arab expansion
📿 Palas of Bengal
Bengal & Bihar (750–1162)
  • Founder: Gopala (1st elected king)
  • Greatest: Dharmapala
  • Patrons of Buddhism
  • Built Vikramshila University
  • Trade with SE Asia & Tibet
🦁 Rashtrakutas
Deccan (757–973 CE)
  • Founder: Dantidurga
  • Greatest: Amoghavarsha I (814–878)
  • Capital: Manyakheta (Malkhed)
  • Built Kailasa Temple (Ellora) — Krishna I
  • Tolerant — patronized Jainism
🧠 MNEMONIC — Tripartite Struggle
"PRG — Pratiharas Rule, Get Kannauj (from) Palas & Rashtrakutas"
P = Pratiharas (North), R = Rashtrakutas (South/Deccan), G = Gurjara (same as Pratihara). All fought over the city of Kannauj.
⚡ Exam Trap Alert
  • Kailasa Temple at Ellora = Rashtrakuta king Krishna I — NOT Amoghavarsha
  • Gopala (Pala founder) is notable as the first elected king in Indian history
  • Arab traveler Sulaiman called Gurjara-Pratihara king "most powerful ruler in India"

🏛 Chola Administration — Most Examined Topic

  • Mandalams = Provinces (6 in number)
  • Valanadus = Districts (Revenue units)
  • Nadu = Sub-district
  • Ur = Village assembly (all castes)
  • Sabha/Mahasabha = Brahmin village assembly
  • Nagaram = Merchant town assembly
  • Village assembly had elected committees (Variyams)
  • Uttaramerur inscription — describes election rules for village assembly (Sembiyan Mahadevi)
  • Military: Velaikkaras = elite bodyguards
  • Kadaram = Kedah (Malaysia) — Rajendra's SE conquest
  • Land grants: Brahmadeya (to Brahmins)
🧠 MNEMONIC — Chola Kings Order
"Very Real Rajendra Kulottungas Ruled Chola"

⚡ Most Asked Chola Facts in SSC

  • Rajendra I = First Indian king to conquer Southeast Asia (Srivijaya Empire)
  • Nataraja (Dancing Shiva) bronze sculpture = Chola contribution
  • Uttaramerur inscription describes the world's earliest democratic election rules
  • Chola navy = most powerful in medieval Asia

Arab & Ghaznavid Invasions
712 CE – 1030 CE
🔥 HIGH YIELD

☽ Muhammad bin Qasim (711–713 CE)

  • Nephew of Hajjaj bin Yusuf (Umayyad governor)
  • Conquered Sindh & lower Punjab (712 CE)
  • Defeated Dahir (Hindu ruler of Sindh)
  • First permanent Muslim rule in India
  • Recalled and executed by Caliph Sulaiman
  • Source: Chachnama (Arabic text)

⚔ Mahmud of Ghazni (971–1030 CE) HOTTEST

  • 17 invasions of India (1000–1027 CE)
  • Main aim: plunder wealth (NOT to establish empire)
  • Destroyed Somnath Temple (Gujarat) in 1025 CE
  • Historian in court: Al-Biruni (wrote Kitab-ul-Hind/Tahqiq-i-Hind)
  • Poet in court: Firdausi (wrote Shahnameh)
  • Title: Idol-Breaker / Yameen-ul-Daula
  • Defeated Jaipal and Anandpal (Hindu Shahi rulers)

⚔ Muhammad Ghori (1173–1206 CE)

  • Full name: Muizz-ud-din Muhammad
  • Goal: establish empire (unlike Mahmud = just plunder)
  • First Battle of Tarain (1191) — Defeated by Prithviraj III
  • Second Battle of Tarain (1192) — Defeated Prithviraj; captured & killed him
  • Battle of Chandawar (1194) — Defeated Jaychandra (Gahadvala king)
  • General Qutb-ud-din Aibak left to govern India
  • Assassinated in 1206 by Ismaili Assassins
  • "Slave" general Aibak became 1st Sultan of Delhi
🧠 MNEMONIC — Mahmud's 17 raids
"Mahmud Came Seventeen Seasons, Plundering Sinfully, And left Al-Biruni Behind"

⭐ Muhammad-bin-Tughlaq (1325–1351) MOST ASKED
"Wisest Fool" · "Mix of Opposites"
⭐ VERY HIGH YIELD

🌟 Character of Muhammad-bin-Tughlaq

Ibn Battuta (Moroccan traveler who visited India 1334): "He was the most generous and magnanimous of men... but he was also the most oppressive and most cruel of men."

Edward Thomas called him: "The Prince of Moneyers". Modern historians call him the "mixture of opposites."

❌ Five "Mad" Experiments (All Failed)

1326–27 — Transfer of Capital
Delhi → Devagiri (renamed Daulatabad). Forced everyone to move ~1500 km. Failed because Delhi was closer to Mongol threat. Capital moved back (1335). OFTEN ASKED
1329–30 — Token Currency
Introduced bronze/copper token coins (instead of silver). Every goldsmith could forge them. Failed completely — treasury emptied. OFTEN ASKED
1333 — Khorasan Expedition
Plan to conquer Khorasan (Iran/Central Asia). Raised huge army — plan abandoned but army remained on payroll for a year, draining treasury.
1337 — Qarachil Expedition
Sent army to Himalayan region (Kumaon/Tibet). Nearly entire army wiped out — rains, disease, guerrilla attacks.
1336 — Doab Taxation
Massively raised taxes in the fertile Doab region during famine. Led to revolts. Left for Deccan; died in Sindh (1351) chasing rebels.

✅ Good Things He Did

  • Set up Diwan-i-Kohi (Agriculture Department) — gave loans (sondhar) to farmers
  • Agricultural experiments in Amir-i-Kohi area (64 km sq near Delhi)
  • Welcomed scholars from Persia, Iraq, Central Asia
  • Ibn Battuta — appointed Qazi of Delhi by Muhammad-bin-Tughlaq
  • Most widespread empire — from Punjab to Bengal to Malabar
  • First sultan to appoint Hindus to high posts

📚 Ibn Battuta in India

  • Moroccan traveler; arrived India 1334 CE
  • Work: Rihla (The Journey) — in Arabic
  • Was appointed Qazi (judge) of Delhi and then sent as ambassador to China
  • Called Delhi "one of the greatest cities in the world"

Akbar's Administrative Reforms MOST ASKED

🏛 Mansabdari System

  • Introduced by Akbar (borrowed from Mongol system)
  • Mansab = rank/office. Every officer had a mansab.
  • Two parts: Zat (personal rank) & Sawar (cavalrymen to maintain)
  • Mansabdars from 10 to 5000 (later up to 7000+)
  • Not hereditary — abolished by death
  • Paid in cash or jagir (revenue assignment)
  • Classification: Amirs (500+), Amir-ul-Umara (top)

🌾 Ain-i-Dahsala (Todar Mal's Revenue System)

  • Revenue minister: Raja Todar Mal
  • Ain-i-Dahsala = 10-year average price system (1580)
  • Land classified into 4 types: Polaj, Parauti, Chachar, Banjar
  • Polaj = cultivated every year (highest revenue)
  • Banjar = fallow for 5+ years
  • Revenue = 1/3 of average produce of 10 years
  • Land measurement: Ilahi Gaz
  • Based on Sher Shah Suri's system

☮ Din-i-Ilahi (1582)

  • Akbar's own "religion" — syncretism of all religions
  • Only 18 followers joined (all courtiers)
  • Only Birbal (Hindu) joined voluntarily among non-Muslims
  • Not a formal religion — more a spiritual order
  • Abolished Jaziya (1564) and Pilgrim Tax (1563)
  • Met scholars at Ibadat Khana (House of Worship, built 1575)

🤝 Akbar's Rajput Policy

  • Policy of reconciliation — married Rajput princesses
  • Rajputs given high mansabs and military commands
  • Abolished Jaziya (tax on non-Muslims)
  • Allowed Rajputs to practice own religions
  • Rajput chiefs: Man Singh, Bhagwan Das, Todar Mal
  • Only Mewar (Rana Pratap) refused to submit

  • True arch + true dome

Provincial Style
  • Gujarat: white marble + Hindu elements
  • Bengal: curved roof (Bangla style)
  • Jaunpur: Screen mosques (Atala Masjid)
  • Kashmir: wooden carvings
  • Malwa: Mandu architecture
Mughal Style
  • Blend: Persian + Central Asian + Indian
  • White marble + red sandstone
  • Pietra dura inlay work
  • Bulbous domes, minarets
  • Char Bagh (4-garden) design
  • Iwan (arched hall), jharokha

⚠ Common SSC Exam Traps & Confusions
⭐ CRITICAL

❌ Don't Confuse These!

  • Kailasa Temple Ellora = Rashtrakuta (Krishna I) — NOT Chalukya
  • Buland Darwaza = Akbar (Gujarat victory) — NOT Shah Jahan
  • Qutb Minar started = Aibak; completed = Iltutmish
  • Humayun's Tomb built by = Haji Begum (his wife, Bega Begum) — NOT Akbar
  • Ain-i-Dahsala = Akbar's, but system by Todar Mal
  • Rupee introduced = Sher Shah Suri — NOT Akbar
  • 3rd Battle of Panipat = 1761 (Marathas vs Afghans) — 1st (1526), 2nd (1556)
  • Itimad-ud-Daulah = built by Nur Jahan for her father — NOT Jahangir

✅ Quick Disambiguation

  • Bibi ka Maqbara = Aurangabad (by Aurangzeb's son) | Taj Mahal = Agra (by Shah Jahan)
  • Iltutmish = son-in-law & successor of Aibak (NOT son)
  • Raziya was Iltutmish's daughter; Iltutmish chose her — not the nobles
  • Malik Kafur = general of Alauddin Khilji (NOT Ghori)
  • Amir Khusrau was disciple of Nizamuddin Auliya (Sufi saint)
  • Babur wrote in Turki (not Persian); Jahangir & Shah Jahan wrote in Persian
  • Sher Shah died at Kalinjar (cannon explosion) — NOT in battle
  • Ibrahim Lodi = only sultan to die in battle (Panipat I, 1526)
