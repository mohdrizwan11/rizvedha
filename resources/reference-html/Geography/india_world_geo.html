Soils of India

🌱 Key Feature

⚫ Black (Regur) Soil — Special Properties

  • Self-ploughing: Develops wide cracks when dry — acts as natural tilling
  • Highly water-retentive: Swells when wet, shrinks when dry
  • Formed from basaltic lava rocks of Deccan Plateau
  • Also called "Cotton Soil" — ideal for rainfed cotton
  • Covers approx. 16.6% of India's total land area
🔥 Special Fact

🟠 Laterite Soil — Unique Characteristics

  • Formed in high temperature + heavy rainfall zones
  • Intense leaching removes lime & silica; leaves iron oxide & aluminium
  • Name from Latin "Later" = Brick
  • Soft when wet, very hard when dry — used in construction
  • Low humus content due to high bacterial activity
  • Tea & coffee grow on high-altitude laterite soils
📍 Most Important

🟡 Alluvial Soil — Key Facts

  • Most widespread — covers ~43% of India's land area
  • Deposited by rivers (Indo-Gangetic Plain)
  • Khadar = new alluvium (lighter, more fertile, near rivers)
  • Bhangar = old alluvium (darker, less fertile, elevated)
  • Rich in potash & lime; deficient in nitrogen & phosphorus
  • Supports India's wheat & rice belts
⚠️ Exam Alert

🔴 Red Soil — Key Facts

  • Red colour due to iron oxide (Fe₂O₃) diffusion
  • Formed from weathering of crystalline igneous rocks
  • Yellow when hydrated (iron in hydrated form)
  • Poor in nitrogen, phosphorus & organic matter
  • Found in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Odisha, S. Bihar, E. Rajasthan
  • Fine-grained red soils in valleys = fertile; coarse upland = poor
🛡️ Soil Conservation Methods ▼

🌳 Vegetative Methods

  • Afforestation: Planting trees to bind soil
  • Shelter belts: Tree rows to reduce wind erosion
  • Strip cropping: Alternate strips of crops
  • Contour farming: Farming along contours of slopes

🔧 Mechanical Methods

  • Contour bunding: Earthen embankments along contours
  • Terraced/Step farming: For hilly slopes
  • Gully plugging: Filling gullies with rocks & boulders
  • Check dams: Slow water runoff

🌱 Other Methods

  • Crop rotation: Maintains soil nutrient balance
  • Mixed farming: Multiple crops together
  • Adding Gypsum: To treat alkaline/saline soils
  • Leaching: Controlled to remove excess salts
🔑

Key fact for SSC: The transfer of minerals from topsoil to subsoil through water is called leaching. Soil erosion on hill slopes is best controlled by contour/linear ploughing.

Agriculture in India

Cropping Seasons, Major Crops, Green Revolution, Irrigation & Modern Practices

⭐

Key Stat: Agriculture accounts for approximately 18% of India's GDP and provides livelihood to about 60% of the population (Economic Survey 2023–24).

🧠 Cropping Seasons Mind Map

☀️ Kharif (Jun–Nov)
❄️ Rabi (Oct/Nov – Mar/Apr)
🌤️ Zaid (Mar–Jun)
☀️ Summer Crop

Kharif (Monsoon Crops)

  • Season: June – November
  • Sown: With onset of monsoon
  • Harvested: September–October
  • Key Crops: Rice, Maize, Sorghum (Jowar), Bajra, Cotton, Groundnut, Jute, Sugarcane, Soybean, Turmeric
  • Require hot, wet climate
❄️ Winter Crop

Rabi (Winter Crops)

  • Season: Oct/Nov – March/April
  • Sown: After retreat of monsoon
  • Harvested: March–April
  • Key Crops: Wheat, Barley, Mustard, Pea, Gram (Chickpea), Linseed, Rapeseed
  • Require cool, dry climate
🌤️ Summer Crop

Zaid (Short Summer Crops)

  • Season: March – June
  • Between Rabi & Kharif
  • Key Crops: Watermelon, Muskmelon, Cucumber, Bitter Gourd, Pumpkin, Moong
  • Grown mainly on irrigated lands

🟢 Green Revolution — Complete Notes ▼

📖 What is the Green Revolution?

  • Launched in India in late 1960s (mid-1960s globally)
  • Introduced by Dr. M.S. Swaminathan (Father of Green Revolution in India)
  • Global pioneer: Dr. Norman Borlaug (Nobel Peace Prize 1970)
  • Aimed to achieve food self-sufficiency after 1965–66 famine

🔬 Key Features

  • HYV (High Yielding Variety) seeds — dwarf wheat & rice
  • Increased use of chemical fertilizers & pesticides
  • Expansion of irrigation infrastructure
  • First benefited Punjab, Haryana, Western UP
  • Focused mainly on wheat & rice

⚠️ Limitations

  • Limited to wheat & rice initially — not pulses/millets
  • Led to soil degradation & waterlogging in Punjab-Haryana
  • Benefits mostly in irrigated areas; rain-fed areas left behind
  • Increased use of groundwater → water table decline
  • Promoted monoculture → loss of biodiversity
💡

Later Revolutions: White Revolution = Operation Flood (Dairy — Dr. Verghese Kurien) | Blue Revolution = Fisheries | Yellow Revolution = Oilseeds | Pink Revolution = Meat & Poultry | Golden Revolution = Fruits & Honey

💧 Irrigation Methods & Types ▼

🌊 Surface Irrigation

  • Canal irrigation: From rivers/reservoirs via channels; largest irrigated area in India
  • Tank irrigation: Common in South India (TN, AP, Karnataka)
  • Suitable for flat, alluvial plains

🔵 Well & Tube-Well Irrigation

  • Most widely used irrigation method in India
  • Depends on groundwater table
  • Common in: UP, Punjab, Rajasthan, Bihar
  • Over-exploitation → declining water tables

💧 Modern Methods

  • Drip irrigation: Water at root level — highly efficient; promoted under PM Krishi Sinchayee Yojana
  • Sprinkler irrigation: Spray water like rain; good for uneven terrain
  • Save 30–50% water compared to flood irrigation
⭐

SSC Fact: An irrigation project is categorized as a Major Project if it covers a Cultivable Command Area (CCA) of above 10,000 hectares.

☢️ Nuclear & Strategic Minerals ▼

☢️ Uranium

  • Jadugoda (Jharkhand) — India's first uranium mine
  • Also in Tummalapalle (AP) — one of world's largest uranium reserves
  • Used in nuclear power plants

⚡ Thorium

  • India has world's largest thorium reserves (~25%)
  • Found in monazite sands of Kerala, TN coasts
  • Palakkad, Quilon (Kerala); Odisha coast
  • Used in India's 3-stage nuclear program

⛽ Natural Gas

  • Krishna-Godavari Basin (KG Basin, AP) — largest reserves
  • Bombay High (Maharashtra)
  • Used as clean fuel (CNG, PNG), fertilizers
  • India promotes gas-based economy under 2030 target

🌏 Asia — Quick Facts

  • Largest continent: 44.6 million km²
  • Most populous: ~4.7 billion people (~60% of world)
  • Contains the highest point (Everest) and lowest point (Dead Sea, −430m)
  • Gobi Desert (largest cold desert); Arabian Desert
  • Major rivers: Indus, Ganges, Brahmaputra, Yangtze, Yellow, Mekong, Irrawaddy
  • Separated from Europe by Ural Mountains

🌍 Africa — Quick Facts

  • 2nd largest continent; crossed by Equator, Tropic of Cancer & Capricorn
  • Sahara = world's largest hot desert
  • Nile = world's longest river (6,650 km)
  • Congo Rainforest = 2nd largest after Amazon
  • Victoria Lake = largest lake in Africa (2nd in world)
  • Rich in minerals: gold, diamonds, platinum (South Africa)

🌎 Americas — Key Contrasts

  • N. America: Rocky, Appalachian Mountains; Great Plains
  • Amazon (S. America): Largest river by volume; 6,400 km
  • Andes: Longest mountain range on land (South America)
  • Angel Falls (Venezuela) = world's highest waterfall
  • Amazon Rainforest = lungs of the Earth (~40% of world's rainforests)

❄️ Antarctica — Exam Facts

  • 5th largest continent; covered by ice (average 1.9 km thick)
  • No permanent human population
  • India's research stations: Maitri, Bharati, Dakshin Gangotri
  • Governed by Antarctic Treaty (1959) — no military, only science
  • Contains ~70% of world's fresh water (as ice)
  • Coldest temperature recorded: −89.2°C (Vostok Station)

🔄 Ocean Currents — Warm & Cold ▼

🔴 Warm Ocean Currents

  • Gulf Stream — Atlantic; warms Western Europe
  • Kuroshio (Japan Current) — Pacific; warms Japan, E. Asia
  • North Atlantic Drift — Extension of Gulf Stream; keeps Europe ice-free
  • Agulhas Current — Indian Ocean; SE Africa coast
  • Brazil Current — S. Atlantic; along Brazil coast
  • El Niño — Warm current that disrupts Pacific weather

🔵 Cold Ocean Currents

  • Labrador Current — N. Atlantic; cools Canada's east coast
  • Canary Current — E. Atlantic; cools NW Africa coast
  • Benguela Current — S. Atlantic; cools SW Africa; creates Namib Desert
  • Humboldt/Peru Current — S. Pacific; cools Peru-Chile coasts
  • California Current — N. Pacific; cools US west coast
  • Oyashio Current — N. Pacific; near Japan
⭐

Key Rule (SSC): Warm currents flow from tropics to poles; Cold currents flow from poles to tropics. Coriolis Force deflects currents to the right in Northern Hemisphere and to the left in Southern Hemisphere. 🎯 Asked in SSC CGL 2013

🌤️ Ocean Currents & Their Climate Influence ▼

🌡️ Warm Currents → Warming Effect

  • Western Europe mild despite high latitude → due to Gulf Stream & North Atlantic Drift
  • Bring moisture → rainfall on adjacent coasts
  • Help in fog formation when meeting cold air

❄️ Cold Currents → Cooling + Desert Formation

  • Atacama Desert (Peru) — caused by Humboldt Current
  • Namib Desert (Africa) — caused by Benguela Current
  • Cold currents cause aridity, fog, cold weather
  • Rich fishing grounds — upwelling brings nutrients to surface

🐟 Marine Resources

  • Fishing grounds: Grand Banks (Atlantic); Georges Bank; North Sea
  • EEZ (Exclusive Economic Zone): 200 nautical miles from coast
  • India's EEZ = 2.02 million km² — rich in fish, oil & gas
  • Polymetallic nodules (manganese, cobalt, nickel) found on ocean floors
💡

El Niño vs La Niña: El Niño = Warm water in E. Pacific → weakens Indian monsoon, causes drought. La Niña = Cold water in E. Pacific → strengthens Indian monsoon, causes floods. Both are ENSO (El Niño Southern Oscillation) events.

Physical Geography — Core Concepts

Earth Structure, Landforms, Mountains, Rivers, Atmosphere — SSC Exam Essentials

🌐 Earth — Basic Facts

  • Shape: Oblate Spheroid (flattened at poles)
  • Age: ~4.6 billion years
  • Area: 510 million km² (71% water, 29% land)
  • Rotation: 23 hrs 56 min (Sidereal Day)
  • Revolution: 365 days 5 hrs 48 min
  • Axis tilt: 23.5° (causes seasons)

🔵 Layers of Earth

  • Crust: 0–70 km; SIAL (Si + Al) under continents; SIMA (Si + Mg) under oceans
  • Mantle: 70–2900 km; Asthenosphere (plastic layer) here
  • Outer Core: 2900–5100 km; liquid iron & nickel
  • Inner Core: 5100–6370 km; solid iron & nickel; densest layer

🏔️ Mountain Types

  • Fold Mountains: Himalayas, Alps, Andes, Rockies (most common)
  • Block Mountains: Vosges (France), Vindhya, Satpura
  • Volcanic Mountains: Mt. Fuji, Mt. Etna, Kilimanjaro
  • Residual Mountains: Aravallis (India's oldest mountain range)

🏞️ Types of Plateaus

  • Intermontane: Tibet (world's highest), Bolivia
  • Continental: Deccan Plateau, Brazilian Plateau
  • Piedmont: Colorado Plateau (USA)
  • Dissected: Shillong, Meghalaya Plateau

🌋 Volcanoes & Earthquake Zones

  • Ring of Fire: Pacific Ocean rim; 75% of world's volcanoes
  • Active volcano: Mt. Etna (Europe's most active); Barren Island (India, Andaman)
  • Dormant: Mt. Fuji, Mt. Kilimanjaro
  • Extinct: Mt. Kenia
  • 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami — triggered by Sumatra earthquake

🏗️ Layers of Atmosphere

  • Troposphere: 0–12 km; weather occurs here; temperature ↓ with altitude
  • Stratosphere: 12–50 km; Ozone layer (15–35 km) absorbs UV rays
  • Mesosphere: 50–80 km; coldest layer; meteors burn here
  • Thermosphere/Ionosphere: 80–700 km; radio waves reflect; Northern Lights
  • Exosphere: 700+ km; outermost; satellites orbit here
⭐

SSC CGL 2013 Asked: The atmospheric layer that absorbs ultraviolet rays of the sun is the Ozonosphere/Stratosphere (Ozone layer). Correct arrangement of layers: Troposphere → Stratosphere → Mesosphere → Ionosphere → Exosphere. 🎯 Direct Exam Question

Climate & Atmosphere

Indian Monsoon, Climate Zones, Wind Systems & Key Atmospheric Phenomena

⭐

Exam Importance: Climatology is the highest-weightage sub-topic — approximately 40% of physical geography questions in SSC CGL come from climate topics.

☔ Indian Monsoon — Key Facts

  • India has a Tropical Monsoon climate type
  • South-West Monsoon (June–September): Main rainy season; brings 75–90% of India's annual rainfall
  • North-East Monsoon (Oct–Dec): Retreating monsoon; rains Tamil Nadu coast
  • Onset: Kerala coast (1st June approximately)
  • Caused by differential heating of land & sea (land heats faster)
  • Mawsynram (Meghalaya) = wettest place in India & world (~11,871 mm annually)

🌡️ Climate Zones of India

  • Tropical Rainforest: Andaman & Nicobar, NE India
  • Tropical Monsoon: Most of India
  • Tropical Savanna: Parts of Deccan
  • Hot Arid Desert: Rajasthan (Thar Desert)
  • Semi-Arid (Steppe): Parts of Rajasthan, AP
  • Subtropical Highland: Sikkim, Himachal (Koppen: Cwa)
  • Alpine/Tundra: High Himalayan region

💨 Global Wind Systems

  • Trade Winds: 0°–30°; blow towards equator; NE in NH, SE in SH
  • Westerlies: 30°–60°; blow towards poles
  • Polar Easterlies: 60°–90°; blow towards equator from poles
  • Jet Stream: High altitude (upper troposphere); affects monsoon
  • Doldrums: Equatorial belt; calm winds
  • Horse Latitudes: ~30°; subtropical high pressure; calm

🌪️ Local Winds — India & World

  • Loo: Hot, dry wind; North India (UP, Haryana) in summers
  • Mango Showers: Pre-monsoon rainfall; Karnataka, Kerala
  • Norwester/Kalbaisakhi: Thunderstorm; WB, Assam (tea cultivation benefit)
  • Mistral: Cold, dry wind; S. France to Mediterranean
  • Foehn/Chinook: Warm, dry wind; descending leeward slopes
  • Harmattan: NE trade wind; W. Africa
  • Sirocco: Hot, dusty; Sahara → Mediterranean

🌊 Humidity & Precipitation Types

  • Convectional Rainfall: Equatorial region; afternoon thunderstorms
  • Orographic Rainfall: Windward side of mountains (Western Ghats, Himalayas)
  • Cyclonic/Frontal Rainfall: Temperate regions; frontal cyclones
  • Cherrapunji vs Mawsynram: Both in Meghalaya; receive world's highest rainfall

🌡️ Temperature & Insolation

  • Insolation: Amount of solar radiation received by Earth's surface
  • Albedo: Reflectivity of surface (snow = high; forest = low)
  • Lapse Rate: Temperature decreases 6.5°C per 1,000m altitude
  • Isohyet: Lines connecting equal rainfall points
  • Isobar: Lines of equal pressure
  • Isotherm: Lines of equal temperature
