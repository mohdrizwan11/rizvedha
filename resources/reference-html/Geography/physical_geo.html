🌋
Fold Mountains
Formed by tectonic compression & folding of rock layers when plates collide
🧱
Block / Fault
Uplifted crustal blocks (Horst) between fault lines; sunken blocks = Graben
🔥
Volcanic
Built by lava, ash & pyroclastic material erupting from magma chambers

Remnants of ancient uplifted surfaces shaped by prolonged erosion
📐 Detailed Characteristics
🌋 Fold Mountains — Earth's Largest Ranges ▼

Formation: Created when two tectonic plates collide (convergent boundary). Sedimentary rocks between plates buckle upward, forming folds (anticlines = crests; synclines = troughs).

  • Young Fold Mountains: Himalayas, Alps, Andes, Rockies — still rising, steep, glaciated
  • Old Fold Mountains: Aravallis (India), Appalachians (USA), Urals — worn down, rounded
🔑
Key Fact
Fold mountains make up the majority of the world's great mountain ranges. All 14 eight-thousanders are in the young Himalayan fold system.
🧱 Block / Fault Mountains — Horst & Graben ▼

Formation: Tensional forces create normal faults. Uplifted block = Horst (block mountain). Sunken block = Graben (rift valley).

  • Indian Examples: Vindhya Range, Satpura Range (horsts)
  • World Examples: Vosges (France), Black Forest (Germany), Sierra Nevada (USA)
  • Rift Valleys: Great Rift Valley (East Africa), Narmada Valley, Rhine Valley
🧠
Memory Trick
"Horst = High up; Graben = Ground low" — Horst is the raised block, Graben is the depression.
🔥 Volcanic Mountains — Fire-built Peaks ▼

Formation: Magma erupts through vents, accumulating lava, ash and pyroclastic material to form a cone-shaped mountain.

Shield Volcano
Gentle slopes; thin basaltic lava; e.g. Mauna Loa (Hawaii)
Stratovolcano
Steep cone; explosive; e.g. Fujiyama, Vesuvius, Pinatubo
  • India's Volcanoes: Barren Island (Andaman — only ACTIVE volcano in South Asia); Narcondam (Andaman — dormant)
  • Deccan Traps: Evidence of massive volcanic activity ~65 million years ago
⚡
SSC CGL Tip
Barren Island volcano is India's ONLY active volcano, located in the Andaman & Nicobar Islands. Extremely frequently tested.
🏝️ Residual / Plateau Mountains — Erosion's Masterpiece ▼

Formation: Formerly elevated land surfaces that have been extensively eroded by water, wind and glaciers, leaving behind hard rock remnants.

  • Indian Examples: Aravalli Hills, Nilgiri Hills, Eastern Ghats (discontinuous)
  • World Examples: Scottish Highlands, Catskill Mountains (USA)

🗺️ Other Major Indian Ranges
🏜️ Aravallis — World's Oldest Mountains ▼

Located in Rajasthan, Haryana, Delhi, Gujarat. Age: ~3.5 billion years — one of the oldest fold mountain systems in the world.

Highest peak: Guru Shikhar (1722m) — Mount Abu, Rajasthan

Acts as a barrier preventing the Thar Desert from spreading eastward.

⚡
SSC CGL Tip
Aravallis = oldest mountain range in India AND among the oldest in the world. Very frequently tested.
🏔️ Vindhyas & Satpura — Central India's Spine ▼

Vindhyas: Central India (MP). Separates North India from Peninsular India. Source of Narmada and Tapti rivers.

Satpura Range: Highest point: Dhupgarh (1350m). Contains Pachmarhi — only hill station of MP.

🌄 Nilgiris — Blue Mountains ▼

Located at the junction of Western and Eastern Ghats. "Nilgiri" = "Blue Mountains" — named after Kurinji flowers (bloom once in 12 years).

Highest peak: Doddabetta (2637m). Famous for Ooty hill station, tea & coffee. Home to Toda tribe.

⚙️ Factors Affecting Climate
🌐 1. Latitude — Distance from Equator ▼

Temperature decreases from the equator to the poles. Near the equator, sunlight strikes at a right angle (concentrated energy). At poles, sunlight is spread over wider areas = less energy per unit area.

⛰️ 2. Altitude — Height above Sea Level ▼

Temperature decreases with altitude at ~6.5°C per 1000 meters (Normal Lapse Rate). Higher locations are cooler because the atmosphere is thinner.

⚡
SSC Tip
Normal Lapse Rate = 6.5°C per 1000m. This rate is frequently asked in numerical form.
🌊 3. Distance from the Sea — Continentality ▼

Areas near the sea = maritime climate — moderate temperatures. Areas far inland = continental climate — extreme temperatures, less rainfall.

Example: Mumbai (coastal) vs Delhi (inland) — Delhi has much more extreme temperatures.

💨 4. Prevailing Winds — Moisture Carriers ▼

Onshore winds (sea to land) = more rainfall. Offshore winds (land to sea) = dry. India's SW monsoon winds are onshore — from the Indian Ocean to the landmass.

🌊 5. Ocean Currents — Temperature Modifier ▼
CurrentTypeEffect
North Atlantic DriftWarmKeeps Western Europe mild
Gulf StreamWarmModerates eastern USA/Canada

⛰️ 6. Relief — Orographic Rain & Rain Shadow ▼

Mountains force air to rise → cools → condenses → heavy rainfall on windward side. Opposite (leeward) side receives little rain = rain shadow.

Example: Western Ghats windward side (Kerala) = very heavy rain; leeward side (Deccan Plateau) = drier. Cherrapunji/Mawsynram = world's wettest places.

🌧️ The Indian Monsoon System
⭐
Key Fact
India receives about 75–90% of its annual rainfall from the SW Monsoon (June–September). The monsoon is the lifeline of India's agriculture.
Southwest Monsoon (June — September)

Mechanism: During summer, land heats faster than sea → low pressure over Indian subcontinent → high pressure over Indian Ocean → moist winds blow from sea to land (SW direction).

🌊
Arabian Sea Branch
Hits Kerala (June 1), Karnataka, Goa, Mumbai; Western Ghats deflects it northward
🌿
Bay of Bengal Branch
Enters NE India, Assam; spreads westward along Gangetic plains
⚡
SSC CGL Tip
SW Monsoon arrives in Kerala around June 1. This date is the most tested monsoon fact.
Retreating / Northeast Monsoon (Oct — Dec)

SW Monsoon withdraws from northwest India in September. As it retreats, picks up moisture over Bay of Bengal → gives rainfall to Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh coast.

Chennai receives most of its annual rainfall from the NE monsoon, NOT the SW monsoon.

Western Disturbances

Extra-tropical cyclones from the Mediterranean Sea, bringing winter rainfall (December–February) to northwestern India. Critical for Rabi crops (wheat, mustard).

☀️ Mediterranean Climate (Cs) — Summer Drought ▼

Location: 30°–45° latitude on WESTERN coasts — California, Spain/Portugal, Mediterranean basin, Chile, South Africa's Cape, SW Australia.

Characteristics: Hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters. Vegetation: Scrub forests (Maquis/Chaparral).

⚡
SSC Tip
Mediterranean climate = Dry summer, Wet winter. This reversal is the defining characteristic frequently asked in exams.
🏜️ Hot Desert Climate (BWh) ▼

Location: 20°–35° latitude — Sahara, Arabian, Thar, Atacama, Australian deserts.

Atacama (Chile) caused by Humboldt cold current; Namib (Africa) by Benguela cold current.

🌲 Tundra Climate (ET) — The Frozen Fringe ▼

Location: North of 60°N — Arctic coast of Russia, Canada, Alaska. Permafrost; very short growing season; mosses, lichens, dwarf shrubs only.

🗺️ Seven Physiographic Divisions of India
⭐
Key Fact
India is divided into 7 major physiographic divisions. This is a standard classification tested in SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, and IB ACIO exams.
1️⃣ The Himalayan Mountains ▼

Cover northern India; young fold mountains (~50 Ma). Three parallel ranges: Himadri, Himachal, Shiwalik. Source of most major Himalayan rivers. Act as a climate barrier against cold Central Asian winds.

2️⃣ The Northern Plains (Indo-Gangetic Plain) ▼

Formed by alluvial deposits of Himalayan rivers. Stretches ~3200 km west to east. Most densely populated region in India. Extremely fertile.

Types of alluvial soil: Khadar (new, fertile, near river) and Bhangar (old, higher ground, contains nodules called "Kankar").

3️⃣ The Peninsular Plateau ▼

Triangular tableland made of ancient crystalline rocks. Includes Deccan Plateau, Central Highlands. One of the world's oldest and most stable landmasses. Rich in minerals (iron ore, coal, manganese, bauxite).

4️⃣ The Indian Desert (Thar) ▼

Located mainly in Rajasthan. World's 7th largest hot desert. Low rainfall (<25cm/year). Luni River drains into Rann of Kutch.

5️⃣ The Coastal Plains ▼
Western Coastal Plain
Narrow (50–80km); Gujarat to Kerala; ports: Mumbai, Goa, Mangaluru, Kochi
Eastern Coastal Plain
Wider (100–130km); WB to TN; Coromandel coast; Chilika Lake, Pulicat Lake
6️⃣ The Islands ▼
Andaman & Nicobar
Bay of Bengal; ~572 islands; capital: Port Blair; India's only active volcano (Barren Island)
Lakshadweep
Arabian Sea; 36 islands (10 inhabited); coral atolls; smallest UT; capital: Kavaratti
7️⃣ The Northeastern Hills (Purvanchal) ▼

Eastern extensions of Himalayas — Patkai Hills, Naga Hills, Manipur Hills, Mizo Hills (Lushai). Form border with Myanmar. Dense forests; high rainfall; biodiversity hotspot.

Most widespread; Indo-Gangetic plains; very fertile; paddy, wheat, sugarcane
⚫
2. Black Soil (Regur)
Maharashtra, MP, Gujarat; best for cotton; retains moisture; from Deccan Trap volcanic rock
🔴
3. Red & Yellow Soil
TN, Karnataka, Odisha, AP; red = iron oxide; less fertile; millets, groundnut
🟤
4. Laterite Soil
Kerala, Karnataka, NE hills; formed by leaching; tea, coffee, rubber; acidic
🏜️
5. Arid / Desert Soil
Rajasthan, Gujarat; low organic content; sandy; very low water retention
🌿
6. Forest & Mountain Soil
Himalayan slopes; acidic; porous; suitable for tea (Darjeeling, Assam)

🌿 Natural Vegetation of India
🌳 1. Tropical Evergreen Forest ▼

Location: Western Ghats (Kerala, Karnataka), NE India, Andaman & Nicobar Islands

Rainfall required: More than 200 cm per year, evenly distributed

Key trees: Ebony, Rosewood, Mahogany, Rubber, Cinchona

🌿 2. Tropical Deciduous Forest (Monsoon) ▼

Location: Most of peninsular India; Rainfall: 75–200 cm

Key trees: Teak (most commercially valuable), Sal, Bamboo, Sandalwood, Neem, Shisham

⚡
SSC Tip
Teak = India's most commercially important timber. Sandalwood (Karnataka) = most valuable for export.
🌵 3. Tropical Thorny / Scrub Forest ▼

Location: Rajasthan, Haryana, semi-arid parts of MP, UP, Gujarat. Rainfall: Less than 75 cm

Key trees: Acacia (babul), Euphorbias, Khejri (State tree of Rajasthan), Dhak, Cactus

🌊 5. Mangrove / Tidal Forest ▼

Location: Sundarbans (WB/Bangladesh), Bhitarkanika (Odisha), Pichavaram (TN)

Sundarbans: World's largest mangrove forest; UNESCO World Heritage Site; home to Royal Bengal Tiger

SSC Tip
