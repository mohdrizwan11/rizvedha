🏗️ Making of the Constitution — Timeline
EXAM TIP Questions on Constituent Assembly composition, committees, and key dates appear in almost every SSC & IB exam!
1934
M.N. Roy first proposed the idea of a Constituent Assembly for India.
1935
Indian National Congress (INC) officially demanded a Constituent Assembly.
1940
August Offer — British accepted the demand for the first time (but with conditions).
1946 (Mar)
Cabinet Mission Plan — Provided scheme for the Constituent Assembly. Very Important
Members elected by indirect election by members of Provincial Assemblies. Total strength: 389 (292 British India + 93 Princely States + 4 Chief Commissioners' Provinces).
9 Dec 1946
First Meeting of Constituent Assembly. Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha — Temporary President (oldest member). Only 211 members attended. Muslim League boycotted.
11 Dec 1946
Dr. Rajendra Prasad elected as Permanent President. H.C. Mukherjee — Vice President. B.N. Rau — Constitutional Adviser.
13 Dec 1946
Objectives Resolution moved by Jawaharlal Nehru. Most Asked
22 Jan 1947
Objectives Resolution adopted (basis of Preamble).
29 Aug 1947
Drafting Committee set up under Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (7 members).
26 Nov 1949
Constitution Adopted — 284 members signed. This date is celebrated as "Constitution Day" (since 2015). Most Asked
Articles on Citizenship, Elections, Provisional Parliament etc. came into force immediately.
26 Jan 1950
Constitution came into full effect. India became a Republic. "Republic Day".
Remaining articles enforced. Total time: 2 years, 11 months, 18 days. Total sessions: 11. Total days: 165.

🌟 Salient Features of Indian Constitution
Salient Features
📏 Lengthiest Written Constitution
  • Originally: 395 Articles, 8 Schedules, 22 Parts
  • Now: ~470+ Articles, 12 Schedules, 25 Parts
  • Detailed provisions = less ambiguity
  • Borrowed from ~10 countries
🔀 Blend of Rigidity & Flexibility
  • Some provisions: Simple majority
  • Some: Special majority (2/3rds)
  • Some: Special majority + state ratification
  • Neither fully rigid nor fully flexible
🏛️ Federal with Unitary Bias
  • Two govts (Centre + State)
  • Division of powers (3 lists)
  • Written constitution, Supremacy
  • BUT: Single citizenship, Emergencies favour Centre
🗳️ Parliamentary System
  • Westminster model (UK)
  • PM is real executive head
  • President is nominal head
  • Collective responsibility to Legislature
⚖️ Independent Judiciary
  • Judicial Review (Art. 13)
  • Security of tenure for judges
  • Separation from Executive
  • Supreme Court = Guardian of Constitution
🔑 Other Key Features
  • Fundamental Rights (Part III)
  • DPSP (Part IV)
  • Fundamental Duties (Part IV-A)
  • Secular State
  • Universal Adult Franchise
  • Single Citizenship
  • Integrated Judiciary
  • Emergency Provisions

📿 The Preamble — Soul of the Constitution
THE CONSTITUTION OF INDIA
PREAMBLE
WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a
[SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC]
and to secure to all its citizens:
JUSTICE — Social, Economic and Political
LIBERTY — of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship
EQUALITY — of status and of opportunity
FRATERNITY — assuring the dignity of the individual and the
unity and integrity of the Nation

IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949,
do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION.
📝 Key Facts about Preamble
  • Based on Objectives Resolution by Nehru (13 Dec 1946)
  • "Socialist", "Secular", "Integrity" added by 42nd Amendment (1976) Most Asked
  • Preamble is NOT a part of Constitution — Berubari Union Case (1960)
  • Preamble IS a part of Constitution — Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973) Most Asked
  • Can be amended under Article 368 (but NOT its basic structure)
  • NOT justiciable — Not enforceable in court
  • NOT a source of power — Only interpretive guide
  • "We the People" — indicates Popular Sovereignty
  • Date in Preamble: 26th November 1949
🔍 Keywords Decoded
Sovereign
India is internally & externally supreme. No external authority controls India.
Socialist
Mixed economy. Democratic socialism (not communist). Added in 1976.
Secular
No state religion. State treats all religions equally. Added in 1976.
Democratic
Popular sovereignty. Govt by the people, for the people, of the people.
Republic
Head of state (President) is elected, NOT hereditary. Unlike UK monarchy.

J L E F
Justice (Social, Economic, Political) • Liberty (Thought, Expression, Belief, Faith, Worship) • Equality (Status, Opportunity) • Fraternity (Dignity, Unity & Integrity)

📝 7th Schedule — Three Lists (Art. 246)

Union List (100 subjects)

Only Parliament can legislate. E.g.: Defence, Atomic Energy, Foreign Affairs, Railways, Banking, Currency, Census, Posts & Telegraph, Citizenship, Inter-State Trade

State List (61 subjects)

Only State Legislature can legislate (normally). E.g.: Police, Public Health, Agriculture, Local Govt, State Public Services, Land, Prisons, Fisheries

Concurrent List (52 subjects)

Both can legislate; Centre prevails in conflict. E.g.: Criminal Law, Marriage, Divorce, Education, Forests, Trade Unions, Electricity, Newspapers, Population Control

KEY Residuary Powers → With Parliament (Centre) — Art. 248. E.g.: Cyber Laws, Space Laws. Borrowed from Canada.

🏛️ Basic Structure Doctrine
LANDMARK Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973) — The Supreme Court (13-judge bench, 7:6 majority) held that Parliament can amend any provision of the Constitution BUT cannot alter/destroy its "Basic Structure".

📋 Detailed Article-wise Breakdown

Right to Equality (Art. 14-18)

Art. 12
Definition of "State" — Includes Govt of India, Parliament, State Govts, Local authorities under control of Govt.
Art. 13
Laws inconsistent with FR — Any law that violates FR is void. Introduces Judicial Review.
Art. 14
Equality before Law + Equal Protection of Laws. Available to ALL persons (citizens + non-citizens).
Art. 15
Prohibition of discrimination on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth. State can make special provisions for women, children, SC/ST/OBC and EWS.
Art. 16
Equality of opportunity in public employment.
Art. 17
Abolition of Untouchability. Enforced via Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955. Absolute FR.
Art. 18
Abolition of Titles. Military & academic distinctions allowed. Bharat Ratna, Padma awards are decorations, NOT titles.

Right to Freedom (Art. 19-22)

Art. 19
6 Freedoms (Only for Citizens):
(a) Freedom of speech & expression
(b) Assemble peacefully without arms
(c) Form associations/unions/cooperative societies
(d) Move freely throughout India
(e) Reside & settle in any part of India
(f) Practice any profession/business/trade
Reasonable Restrictions can be imposed on valid grounds like public order or sovereignty.
Art. 20
Protection from conviction: (1) No ex-post-facto law (2) No double jeopardy (3) No self-incrimination. Cannot be suspended during Emergency.
Art. 21
Right to Life & Personal Liberty. Available to ALL persons. Includes Right to Privacy (Puttaswamy case). Cannot be suspended during Emergency.
Art. 21A
Right to Education — Free & compulsory education for children 6-14 years. Added by 86th Amendment (2002).
Art. 22
Protection against arrest & detention. Allows for procedural checks and governs Preventive Detention.

Right Against Exploitation (Art. 23-24)

Art. 23
Prohibition of trafficking, begar (forced labour). Available against State and private actors.
Art. 24
Prohibition of child labour below 14 years in hazardous employment.

Right to Freedom of Religion (Art. 25-28)

Art. 25
Freedom of conscience, profession, practice & propagation of religion. Sikhs carrying Kirpan is protected under this.
Art. 26
Right to manage religious affairs and establish institutions.
Art. 27
No person shall be compelled to pay taxes for promotion of any particular religion.
Art. 28
No religious instruction in fully State-funded educational institutions.

Cultural & Educational Rights (Art. 29-30)

Art. 29
Any section of citizens with distinct language, script or culture has right to conserve it.
Art. 30
Minorities (religious & linguistic) have right to establish & administer educational institutions.

Right to Constitutional Remedies (Art. 32)

Art. 32
"Heart and Soul of the Constitution" — Dr. Ambedkar. Power to move SC for enforcement of FR through 5 writs. Itself a FR.
📜 The 5 Constitutional Writs Very Frequently Asked
KEY DIFFERENCE Art. 32 → SC can issue writs ONLY for FR violations.
Art. 226 → HC can issue writs for FR violations AND for "any other purpose" (wider scope).
1. Habeas Corpus
"To have the body"
Issued against public or private authority that has illegally detained a person. Orders the detainer to produce the person. Bulwark of individual liberty.
2. Mandamus
"We command"
Commands a public official, body or inferior court to perform a public statutory duty they have failed or refused to execute.
3. Prohibition
"To forbid"
Issued by higher court to an inferior court or quasi-judicial tribunal to prevent it from exceeding its legal jurisdiction. Precise preventative action.
4. Certiorari
"To be certified"
Issued by higher court to lower court or administrative authority to quash an order passed in excess of jurisdiction or violating principles of natural justice.
5. Quo Warranto
"By what authority"
Questions the legal authority of a person holding a substantive public office created by the Constitution or statute. Can be filed by any public citizen.

🇮🇳 Fundamental Duties (Part IVA, Art. 51A)
KEY FACTS Added by 42nd Amendment (1976) on recommendation of Swaran Singh Committee. Originally 10 duties. 11th duty added by 86th Amendment (2002). Borrowed from USSR. Non-justiciable. Apply only to Citizens.
51A(a)
Abide by the Constitution, respect National Flag & National Anthem
51A(b)
Cherish noble ideals of freedom struggle
51A(c)
Uphold & protect sovereignty, unity & integrity of India
51A(d)
Defend the country & render national service when called
51A(e)
Promote harmony & common brotherhood; renounce practices derogatory to dignity of women
51A(f)
Value & preserve rich heritage of composite culture
51A(g)
Protect & improve natural environment — forests, lakes, rivers, wildlife
51A(h)
Develop scientific temper, humanism & spirit of inquiry

🏛️ Union Government & Parliament

President, VP, PM, Council of Ministers, Parliament (LS & RS), Judiciary — Complete Coverage

🇮🇳 President of India (Art. 52-62, 72, 123)

Election (Art. 54, 55)

  • Electoral College: Elected members of LS + RS + State Legislative Assemblies (No nominated members, no MLCs)
  • Method: Proportional Representation by means of Single Transferable Vote
  • Disputes: Exclusively decided by the Supreme Court (Art. 71)

Qualifications (Art. 58)

  • Citizen of India, Age ≥ 35 years
  • Qualified for election as a member of Lok Sabha
  • Should not hold any office of profit

Term & Impeachment

  • Term: 5 years. Eligible for re-election.
  • Impeachment (Art. 61): Only for "violation of Constitution". Requires 2/3rd majority of total membership of each house.
  • No President has ever been impeached in India.

🔑 Veto Powers of President

Absolute Veto
Withholding assent completely. Bill dies.
Suspensive Veto
Returning the bill for reconsideration. Overridden by simple repassage. Not applicable to Money Bills.
Pocket Veto
Taking no action indefinitely. Zail Singh used it for Indian Post Office Amendment Bill, 1986.

🟢 Lok Sabha (Lower House)
  • Max strength: 550 elected members (Anglo-Indian reserved seats removed by 104th Amendment).
  • Election: Direct election via First-Past-The-Post system.
  • Term: 5 years (subject to premature dissolution).
  • Minimum age: 25 years.
  • Special powers: Money Bills can only originate here. No-Confidence motion can only be moved here.
🔴 Rajya Sabha (Upper House)
  • Max strength: 250 (238 elected from states/UTs + 12 nominated by President).
  • Term: Permanent body. 1/3rd members retire every 2 years; individual terms are 6 years.
  • Minimum age: 30 years.
  • Presiding Officer: Vice President of India acts as ex-officio Chairman.
  • Special powers: Art. 249 (Authorize Parliament to legislate on State List); Art. 312 (Create All-India Services).
📋 Important Parliamentary Committees

Public Accounts Committee (PAC)

22 members (15 from Lok Sabha, 7 from Rajya Sabha). Audits the appropriation accounts and reports of the CAG. Conventionally chaired by an opposition member.

Estimates Committee

30 members, **drawn exclusively from the Lok Sabha**. It is the largest standing financial committee of Parliament, analyzing budget expenditures.

⚖️ Supreme Court of India (Art. 124-147)

Composition & Tenure

  • Current sanctioned strength: **CJI + 33 other judges** (Total 34).
  • Judges hold office until the age of **65 years**.
  • Appointed by the President via the Collegium system.

Jurisdictions

Original (Art. 131)
Intergovernmental disputes (Centre vs States or State vs State).
Writ (Art. 32)
Exclusive enforcement of Fundamental Rights.
Advisory (Art. 143)
President requests legal opinion. SC advice is **non-binding**.

🗺️ State & Local Government

Governor, CM, State Legislature, Centre-State Relations, Panchayati Raj, Municipalities & Constitutional Bodies

🏛️ Governor (Art. 153-162)
  • Appointed by the President; serves during the **pleasure of the President**.
  • Constitutional executive head of the State.
  • **Art. 161:** Pardoning power excludes death sentences and court-martial orders.
  • **Art. 213:** Power to promulgate state ordinances when legislature is recessed.
👔 Chief Minister (Art. 163-164)
  • Real executive head of the state administration.
  • Appointed by the Governor as head of the majority legislative party.
  • State Council of Ministers cannot exceed **15%** of the legislative assembly's total strength (91st Amendment).
🏘️ Local Self-Government Foundations

73rd Amendment (Panchayats)

Added **Part IX** and the **11th Schedule** (29 subjects). Establishes a mandatory 3-tier structure: Gram, Intermediate, and District levels. Mandates 1/3rd reservation for women.

74th Amendment (Municipalities)

Added **Part IX-A** and the **12th Schedule** (18 subjects). Governs urban local bodies structured into Nagar Panchayats, Municipal Councils, and Municipal Corporations.
